## Group 1 — `separate` and `headOfHousehold`, in nine states at once

The largest group by far, and the one with the clearest single fix.

| state | the cells nothing pins |
| --- | --- |
| California | `separate` in the exemption credit (`$153`), the renter's credit (`$1,250`), the AGI limit (`$252,203`) and the standard deduction (`$5,706`) |
| Maryland | `headOfHousehold` in the itemized-deduction limit (`$200,000`), the senior credit (`$1,750`), the poverty credit's income limit (`$150,000`) and the exemption (`$6,700`) |
| Ohio | `headOfHousehold` in the business-income limit (`$250,000`) and the exemption (`$2,400`) |
| Utah / Pennsylvania | `separate` in three credit tables (`$18,213`, `$16,000`, `$27,000`) |
| New Jersey | `separate` in the exemption (`$1,000`) and the retirement exclusion (`$10,000`) |
| New York | `separate` in the standard deduction (`$8,000`) |
| Massachusetts | `single`, `joint` and `headOfHousehold` in the rent deduction cap (`$4,000`) — here it is the SEPARATE one that is tested |
| Georgia / flat states | `separate` at `$250,000`, `$65,897`, `$67,610`; `headOfHousehold` in two exemption tables |
| Virginia | *(fixed on Day 33 — see `test/virginia-age-deduction.test.js`)* |

**THE RULE: a `byStatus` table is tested by the statuses somebody filed, and
nobody files separately.** Married-filing-separately and head-of-household are the
two statuses a test author reaches for last, and in nearly every state they carry
their own numbers rather than a derivation of somebody else's. Massachusetts is the
proof that this is about attention rather than about the statuses: there the
separate cell is the tested one and the other three are not.

**The fix is one test, and it is the state analogue of Day 33's federal
`every-year.test.js`:** a status sweep that, for every state and every rule with a
`byStatus` table, asserts a level for every status the state's own form has. Note
the trap — `formStatuses` has been on the plan for three days for exactly this
reason, because a state whose form has no head-of-household column must not be
asserted to have a figure for it. **Do `formStatuses` first; this is what it is
for.**

## Group 2 — year-conditional branches, taken in one direction only

| file | line | the branch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `flat-states.js` | 102 | `year === 2025 ? 24000 : 30000` |
| `flat-states.js` | 157, 169, 205, 287 | `year >= 2024` / `>= 2026` / `>= 2025` spreads |
| `flat-states.js` | 664 | `minimumAge: year === 2025 ? 80 : 81` |
| `maryland.js` | 280–282, 304 | the poverty guideline, and a 2026 note block |
| `ohio.js` | 87, 237, 244 | `year >= 2026 ? 500_000 : 750_000` |
| `new-jersey.js` | 104, 229 | `year >= 2026 && year <= 2028 ? 1.25 : 1` |

Mutating the year in the condition survives, which means **only one side of the
branch is ever evaluated by a test.** This is the same disease Day 33 found and
fixed in the federal package — the suite is a suite for one year — reappearing in
the state package in a different shape, as a conditional rather than as a
per-year file.

New Jersey's is the one to read twice: `1.25` applies **only** in 2026–2028, so it
is a window with two edges, and nothing tests either of them.

**The fix:** `test/year-over-year.test.js` already exists and already runs every
state across years. It needs to assert a LEVEL on each side of each conditional
rather than that the years differ — which is Day 32's rule again, because "2026
differs from 2025" is a difference and is blind to both.

right output is an assertion that it cannot be reached, as

## Group 4 — the ones to check for deadness before testing

`flat-states.js` 733–735 (`25500`/`12750`/`19125`) and `maryland.js` 244–245's
repeated `1_750`. Repetition across statuses with no test anywhere is the shape of
a parameter nothing reads. **Check reachability first.** Writing a test for a dead
parameter is worse than leaving it, because it asserts that something matters when
it does not.

## How to work this list

1. `formStatuses` first — Group 1 needs it and cannot be written safely without it.
2. Group 1 as one status sweep. Largest return per hour by a wide margin.
3. Group 2 by strengthening `year-over-year.test.js` from differences to levels.
4. Group 3 state by state, New York first, since it also closes an aged item.
5. Group 4 last, and only after a reachability probe.

Re-run after each and update the table at the top:

```sh
node tools/mutation/mutate.mjs packages/us-state-tax --workers 4 \
  --skip localities/ohio.js,localities/ohio-school-districts.js,localities/indiana.js,localities/michigan.js,localities/maryland.js,localities/new-york.js,localities/counties.js
```

When the count reaches zero, add `--max-survivors 0` to the `state` job in
`.github/workflows/mutation.yml`, as `us-federal-tax` already has. Until then that
job reports and does not gate — a permanently red workflow teaches the reader to
ignore Actions, which costs more than the thing it complains about.
