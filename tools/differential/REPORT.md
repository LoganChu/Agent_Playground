## Explained

- **38** — A DEFECT THERE, and it was a defect HERE until Day 27. § 24(b)(2) sets the phase-out threshold at "$400,000 in the case of a joint return, and $200,000 in any other case". A QUALIFYING SURVIVING SPOUSE DOES NOT FILE A JOINT RETURN - § 2(a) hands them the joint RATE SCHEDULE under § 1(a) and says nothing about any threshold - so the figure is $200,000, and Schedule 8812 line 9 prints the same split in two lines: "Married filing jointly-$400,000" and "All other filing statuses-$200,000". PolicyEngine-US carries $400,000 for this status, so at $250,000 and $300,000 of wages it gives a widow with one child the full $2,200 and this package gives nothing. NOTE WHICH CASE IS MISSING FROM THIS LIST: at $450,000 the two engines AGREE at $0, because that income clears the joint threshold as well. That is the whole reason the $450,000 shape exists - without it, a report of two disagreeing incomes could not distinguish "this package now uses $200,000" from "this package lost the credit for some other reason". This package carried $400,000 too, with a comment calling the question UNRESOLVED on the grounds that irs.gov is blocked from the build sandbox and PolicyEngine agreed; the statutory sentence settles it unaided and was quoted in that comment. See packages/us-federal-tax/test/surviving-spouse.test.js, which walks the parameter tree and requires every status-keyed table in the package to declare its statutory grouping. (largest: `surviving-spouse-high-AZ-250000-0-0-0-0`, -$2,200.00)

- **19** — § 32(d), and a different reading of the ARPA amendment. A married filer who files separately gets no earned income credit under § 32(d)(1) unless § 32(d)(2) applies — which needs the filer to have lived with a qualifying child for more than half the year AND either to have had a different principal abode from the spouse for the last six months or to be legally separated and not a member of the same household. PolicyEngine-US turns a single parameter, `eligibility.separate_filer`, true from 2021 and allows the credit to EVERY separate filer. This package requires the facts: `separatedFromSpouse` plus a qualifying child, and the case grid supplies neither, so it computes zero. Neither model is reading the statute wrongly — one is assuming the exception applies and the other is assuming it does not, and nothing on a return either model was given says which. $1,053.62 for one child at $45,000 in 2026.
