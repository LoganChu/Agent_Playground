- **19** — NOT UPDATED THERE, plus PROVISIONAL FIGURES here. Two facts, and between them they account for every Maryland difference in this grid to the cent — verified by running this package with both of PolicyEngine's figures, after which 22 of the 23 Maryland cases agreed exactly. (1) ALLEGANY COUNTY raised its rate from 3.03% to 3.20% for 2026. This package carries the new rate; PolicyEngine's county table cites the 2025 resident booklet and stops there, so it charges 2025's. That is 0.17 of a point of taxable income, which is why this entry is bounded by a SHARE OF INCOME rather than by a dollar figure: the same one fact is $43.76 on a $30,000 household and $678.70 on a $400,000 one. (2) The 2026 standard deduction. HB 352 replaced the 15%-of-AGI formula with a flat $3,350/$6,700 indexed by the chained CPI from 2026; this package carries the 2025 figure forward and flags it provisional, and PolicyEngine uprates it to $3,400/$6,850. That is at most $150 of deduction, about $6 of state and county tax at the top rate. (largest: `single-worker-MD-400000-0-0-0-0`, $678.70)
- **18** — PROVISIONAL FIGURES. California's 2026 brackets, standard deduction and exemption credits are the 2025 published amounts carried forward; the FTB indexes them to the CCPI after this was written. PolicyEngine projects them. (largest: `retired-couple-CA-0-90000-40000-0-0`, $359.05)

- **2** — HARNESS, in the state. The same fact as the two federal entries above, one level down. At $400,000 PolicyEngine's household ITEMISES federally - its only itemised deduction being the state income tax it is in the middle of computing - and two states then follow that federal election, in both directions. VIRGINIA requires a federal itemiser to itemise on the state return (Va. Code § 58.1-322.03(1)) and subtracts state income tax from the itemised total, which in this harness leaves ZERO, so PolicyEngine gives the filer no deduction at all where this package gives the $8,750 standard one: $503.13 at 5.75%. GEORGIA does the same under § 48-7-27(a)(1), allowing $21,965 of itemised deductions against this package's $15,000 standard one, and then pays $300 a taxpayer for having itemised - the eligible itemizer credit of O.C.G.A. § 48-7-27.1, which IS modelled here from v0.22.0 and which test/georgia-itemizer-credit.test.js pins. It does not fire in this grid because the harness's own federal return takes the standard deduction, so `federal.deductionKind` is `standard` on every case in it. $347.55 of deduction plus $300 of credit is the $647.55. Neither difference can be closed from this harness, which supplies no itemised deductions on purpose: feeding a state tax back into the federal return is circular. NOTE the cite: this entry said § 48-7-29.23 until Day 26, copied from PolicyEngine-US's own variable file. No such section exists - their PARAMETER file has it right. (largest: `single-worker-GA-400000-0-0-0-0`, $647.55)

- **1** — A DEFECT THERE. A retired couple both 70 with $40,000 of Social Security and no other income: Indiana exempts the benefit, so federal AGI is zero and the $5,000 of exemptions takes Indiana AGI to MINUS $5,000. The whole difference is Marion County's 2.02% applied to that negative figure: `in_county_tax` is `rate * in_agi` with no floor, so PolicyEngine has an Indiana county paying a retiree $101 a year. It floors the STATE tax at zero on the same figure, which is what makes this an oversight rather than a reading of the statute. The other half of this difference used to be the unified tax credit for the elderly, IC 6-3-3-9 - $140, refundable, and the whole of an Indiana return for a household living on Social Security. It is modelled here from v0.22.0 and the two engines now agree on it to the cent, which is why this entry's bound fell from $250 to $110. (largest: `retired-couple-IN-0-0-40000-0-0`, $101.00)
